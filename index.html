Draw a Stickman: Epic 3

Level Design Case Study

Role: Lead Level Designer

Overview: As Lead Level Designer, I designed and led a team building nine narrative-driven levels for Draw a Stickman: Epic 3, teaching mechanics through environmental design rather than explicit instruction. Below: the level design process for Level 2 Stage 1, from first sketch to team-ready documentation.

Design Problem

Introduce new Wind Pencil puzzles, a new enemy (Trolls), and a new hazard (corrupting Ooze). Teach players how to overcome new challenges through environmental storytelling and short cutscenes.

1. Initial Concept Sketch

Mapping the critical path alongside every system that touches it: monster behavior, tool interactions, hidden rewards, story logic.

2. Critical Path Refinement

Isolating the actual route a player walks and pressure-testing pacing: encounter timing, fail-safe moments, difficulty ramp into Stage 2.

3. Formal Level Design Document

Turning the sketch into a doc the wider team could build against: key concepts, puzzle types, the monster pathfinding and behavior.

4. Greyblocking and Prototyping

Once the design was reviewed and approved by the team, I began greyblocking out the level and prototyping the puzzles and enemies in Unity. We used a visual scripting tool for this project that allowed the level designers to work with the game's code to create unique puzzles, hazards and enemies.

Design Highlights

  • Teaching through consequence, not instruction: The Wind Pencil's toolkit is taught through environmental interactions the player discovers and repeats.
  • Systemic monster behavior: Trolls chase Butterflies, and Butterflies are drawn to Sun Flowers; players learn to manipulate a simple ecosystem.
  • Layered risk without punishment: Ooze, fire, and Trolls all have soft fail states, so mistakes teach instead of frustrate.

What I'd Change Today

There were changes made from the initial design before launch that improved pacing: One big change was moving the Wind Pencil introduction to the end of Level 1x2, which gave players more time to explore interactions. There is still room for improvement with the pacing of this level; I would reduce the frequency of interactions, hazards, enemies, and puzzles, saving reinforcement of new concepts for later levels.

Canna Farm

UX Case Studies

Role: UX design, Product Manager

Overview: A weed-farming management game blending casual and idle mechanics. Led design and production during live-ops, focusing on user onboarding, UX and progression system updates to improve KPIs.

Below are two features I designed on CannaFarm: the Notice Board and the Dispensary.

Case Study: Notice Board Feature

Feature Overview

The Notice Board centralizes game updates, social interactions, and promotional offers in one place. It was built to improve player retention, engagement, and monetization.

Problem Statement and Goals

Problem: Players lacked a unified interface for accessing social features, game updates, and promotions.

  1. Increase player retention through timely updates.
  2. Enhance social connectivity with features like Discord integration.
  3. Boost revenue through targeted special offers.

Key Features

  • Social Page: The default page for social interactions, including Discord integration and social boosts.
  • News Page: Highlights updates, events, and promotional offers.
  • Popups on Login: Contextual multi-page popups for timely updates.

Design Process

  1. Research: Identified player pain points and gathered stakeholder feedback.
  2. Screen flows: Created detailed wireframes and mapped the user's path.
  3. Iteration: Refined navigation flow and functionality.
  4. Analytics: Tracked retention, engagement, and revenue.

KPIs: Increase social interactions and task completions, and improve conversion rates for special offers.

Case Study: Dispensary Feature

Feature Overview

The Dispensary is a 3D in-game hub where players take part in the CannaFarm economy by buying and selling items. Players can sell products to other players and to visiting NPC Patrons. The feature was meant to draw players into the online community and grow CannaFarm's social features.

Problem Statement and Goals

Problem: Players lacked a compelling, immersive space for engaging with the CannaFarm economy.

  1. Create a virtual space to enhance player engagement.
  2. Promote buying and selling of player-produced items.
  3. Strengthen the in-game economy through interaction with NPCs and other players.

Key Features

  • 3D Hub: A virtual cannabis café for buying and selling player items.
  • NPC Transactions: Sell items to visiting NPC Patrons.
  • Drag-and-Drop Interface: Simplified UI for selecting and selling items.
  • Player Economy: Highlights and encourages trade of player-produced goods.

KPIs: Improve retention, incentivize players to buy and sell in-game items with other players, and increase engagement with CannaFarm's in-game economy.

Critter Fu

Product Design Case Study

Critter Fu was an unreleased mini-game prototype for the Box Critters virtual world. I do not claim copyright over any of the Box Critters characters or artwork shown here, or over any imagery used as placeholders in the wireframes; all belong to their respective owners and appear for portfolio purposes only.

Role: Product Designer, Product Manager

Overview: Web-based mini-game prototype made for the Box Critters virtual world. I owned the design for every part of the game: UX, systems, and gameplay. As Product Manager, I used a lightweight agile process to guide the team through rapid prototyping, manage project scope, and maintain client and stakeholder relationships.

Concept

Box Critters Kung Fu is a kung-fu-themed, multiplayer puzzle fighting game. Players fight martial arts battles by dragging and connecting tiles on a grid, launching attacks and counters in a fast match-3 format.

  • Target market: Players aged 8+, with an emphasis on engagement and fast-paced gameplay.
  • Session length: 3–5 minutes per game, balancing accessibility and depth.
  • Platform: HTML5 stand-alone mini-game.
  • Monetization: Free-to-play, with room to monetize through progression and customization.

Core Loop

Battles earn experience and rank. Ranking up unlocks new Kung Fu stances and moves, which give players more power and strategy to take back into battle.
