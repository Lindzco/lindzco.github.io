Draw a Stickman: Epic 3 — Level Design Case Study

Draw a Stickman: Epic 3 — Narrative-driven puzzle level design. Led design on nine levels blending storytelling, exploration, and environmental puzzle mechanics.

As Lead Level Designer, I designed and led a team building nine narrative-driven levels for Draw a Stickman: Epic 3, teaching mechanics through environmental design rather than explicit instruction. Below: one level's process, from first sketch to team-ready documentation.

Design Problem

Introduce the Wind Pencil tool, a new enemy (Trolls), and a new hazard (corrupting Ooze) — entirely through environmental design, no tutorial popups.

1. Initial concept sketch

Mapping the critical path alongside every system that touches it — monster behavior, tool interactions, hidden rewards, story logic.

2. Critical path refinement

Isolating the actual route a player walks and pressure-testing pacing: encounter timing, fail-safe moments, difficulty ramp into Stage 2.

3. Formal level design document

Turning the sketch into a doc the wider team could build against — key concepts, puzzle types, the monster-avoidance loop.

Design Highlights

  • Teaching through consequence, not instruction — the Wind Pencil's toolkit is taught entirely through environmental interactions the player discovers and repeats.
  • Systemic monster behavior — Trolls chase Butterflies, Butterflies are drawn to Sun Flowers; players learn to manipulate a simple ecosystem, not memorize rules.
  • Layered risk without punishment — Ooze, fire, and Trolls all have soft fail states, so mistakes teach instead of frustrate.

What I'd Change Today

I'd space out the Wind Pencil, hazard, enemy, and puzzle introductions more — the first third of the level stacks a lot of new information at once. I'd also want playtest data on whether the hidden mushroom-to-tree-branch bounce reads clearly enough on its own.
